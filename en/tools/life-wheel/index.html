Evidence article

What is the Wheel of Life, and what is scientifically grounded here?

The Wheel of Life is a coaching reflection exercise, not a standardized psychometric test by itself. On this site, the seven current-state wellbeing questions are anchored in the Personal Wellbeing Index – Adult (PWI-A). The coaching layers — desired level, importance, influence, readiness and priorities — are kept separate from the PWI-A score and should not be treated as psychometrically validated measures.

What the evidence supports

  • The seven current-state questions in this version are anchored in the Personal Wellbeing Index – Adult (PWI-A).
  • PWI-A is a short adult self-report measure of subjective wellbeing / life satisfaction across seven domains, described in sources such as PhenX and the subjective wellbeing literature.
  • The seven-domain mean can be expressed on a 0–100 scale by multiplying the 0–10 mean by 10; overall life satisfaction is reported separately.

What it does not support

  • That the classic coaching Wheel of Life is itself a validated psychometric instrument.
  • That this site’s coaching layers (desired level, importance, influence, readiness, priorities) inherit PWI-A psychometric validation.
  • That sample-specific Persian studies equal population-wide Iranian norms or validate the full combined coaching tool.

What is this tool?

In coaching, the Wheel of Life is typically a reflective visualisation exercise: looking across life domains together so the current picture and conversation starting point become clearer.

This website version keeps that reflective purpose, anchors the current-state core in seven PWI-A-aligned questions, and keeps the coaching layers separate from the psychometric score.

What is scientifically grounded — and what is not?

The evidence base mainly applies to the PWI-A core, not automatically to the full combined coaching tool. PhenX describes PWI-A as a seven-item, self-administered measure and cites the International Wellbeing Group 5th edition (2013).

Cummins et al. (2003) is a foundational peer-reviewed source for the Australian Unity Wellbeing Index / subjective wellbeing index. Tomyn, Fuller-Tyszkiewicz and Cummins (2013) provide psychometric evidence on PWI equivalence across adult and school-child versions.

Studies in specific Iranian samples have reported supportive reliability/validity findings for Persian versions; that is not the same as national population norms or universal Persian validation of this combined tool.

What does it measure?

The seven core domains are: standard of living, health, achievement in life, personal relationships, personal safety, community connectedness, and future security.

Each domain asks perceived satisfaction on a 0–10 scale. Overall life satisfaction is asked separately and is not included in the seven-domain average.

Additional layers — desired level, importance, influence/agency, readiness, priorities and coaching reflections — prepare conversation and are not part of the PWI-A score.

How does scoring work?

Each of the seven domains is scored 0–10. The seven-domain mean can be multiplied by 10 to express a 0–100 score — the common PWI-A reporting convention.

Overall life satisfaction is shown separately and is not folded into the seven-domain mean.

Coaching indicators are not combined with the scientific score and do not claim independent psychometric validation.

How should results be interpreted responsibly?

The result is a current snapshot of perceived satisfaction — not a verdict on your worth, personality or mental health.

A lower score in one domain is not automatically the coaching priority; weigh importance, influence, readiness and personal meaning together.

If you have serious mental-health concerns, this tool is not a substitute for specialist assessment.

How can it be used in coaching?

Use the result as a conversation starting point: where energy exists, where tension shows, and which one to three domains deserve attention over the next three months.

Keep the PWI-A score intact and use the coaching layer to choose a topic — not to “fix” the score.

If you want to explore working together, use the start path; the tool is still useful for independent reflection.

Limits — what it must not be used for

It is not a medical or psychological diagnosis, disorder screening, or a substitute for mental-health specialist assessment.

It should not be used for hiring decisions, ranking people, or claiming that the classic Wheel of Life as a whole is scientifically validated.

Sample-based Persian evidence should not be generalised as national norms or as validation of the combined coaching layers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Wheel of Life?
The Wheel of Life is a coaching reflection exercise that places life domains side by side so the current picture and conversation starting point become clearer. By itself it is not a standardized psychometric test.
Is the Wheel of Life scientifically validated?
The classic Wheel of Life coaching exercise is not a standardized psychometric instrument. On this site, the scientific evidence mainly applies to the seven current-state questions anchored in PWI-A — not to the additional coaching layers.
What is the PWI-A?
The Personal Wellbeing Index – Adult is a short adult self-report measure of subjective wellbeing / life satisfaction across seven domains: standard of living, health, achievement in life, personal relationships, personal safety, community connectedness and future security.
How is the PWI-A score calculated?
Each domain is scored 0–10. The seven-domain mean can be multiplied by 10 for a 0–100 scale. Overall life satisfaction is reported separately and is not included in that average.
How is this version different from a classic Wheel of Life exercise?
A classic wheel is usually a visual reflection exercise. Here the seven current-state questions are anchored in PWI-A, and desired level, importance, influence, readiness and priorities stay separate from the PWI-A score.
How can I use the result in coaching?
Bring the result as a starting picture: strengths, tensions and one to three chosen priorities. Do not treat the score as a personality label; use the coaching layer to choose what deserves attention.
Where are my responses stored?
Responses are not sent to a server. They stay in this browser on this device. Mehdi cannot see local results unless you copy or share a summary yourself.
