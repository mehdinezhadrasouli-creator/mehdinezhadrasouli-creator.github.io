HR & Organization

People questions are rarely only people questions

Before treating a leadership or performance issue as an individual problem, look at the role, system, relationships and business context around it.

22 August 20267 min readPractice essay

Core question

At what level does this problem actually live?

In brief

Before treating a leadership or performance issue as an individual problem, look at the role, system, relationships and business context around it. The practical invitation is to slow the diagnosis, distinguish observation from assumption and choose a response that fits the real level of the problem rather than its first label.

Evidence note

Distilled from more than 15 years of cross-sector HR leadership and 700+ hours of coaching practice. No client story or identifiable client material is used.

01

The first version of the problem

Organizations often describe a problem through a person: a leader is not strategic enough, a team is not accountable enough, or a high performer is not ready for the next role. The description may be accurate, but it is rarely complete.

A person is visible. The system around that person is easier to miss. That makes individual development an attractive first response even when the conditions sustaining the problem sit elsewhere.

02

The wider field

A leadership challenge may also involve unclear decision rights. A performance concern may also involve competing incentives, missing capability or an overloaded role. A retention concern may carry information about work design, management quality or the employee experience.

This does not remove individual responsibility. It places that responsibility in context, so the intervention is aimed at the real mix of person, role and system.

03

Find the question underneath

Before choosing coaching, training, advice or structural change, slow down the diagnosis. Ask what is observable, what the current system rewards, what the role requires and which relationships shape the outcome.

The quality of the intervention depends on the quality of the question that comes before it.

04

Choose the intervention after the diagnosis

If the constraint sits mainly in awareness, judgment or behaviour, coaching may be useful. If the role is contradictory, decision rights are unclear or incentives reward the wrong outcome, individual coaching alone is unlikely to be enough.

A more responsible response may combine leadership work with role clarification, stakeholder alignment, capability building or a change to the surrounding system. The aim is not a more elaborate solution; it is a solution matched to the level of the problem.

Questions to carry forward

  1. 01What are we observing, and what are we merely labelling?
  2. 02What in the role or system may be sustaining this pattern?
  3. 03Which part of the change belongs to the individual, and which part belongs to the organization?
