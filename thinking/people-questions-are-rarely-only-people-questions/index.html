Coaching & Leadership

When you cannot change the system, where can you start?

Not everything is under our control. Coaching can help separate what is within a leader’s control from what they can influence, and turn a large problem into choices and actions that are still available.

7 September 20266 min readPractice essay

Core question

When a manager, structure or organizational condition will not change, where can the client still begin?

In brief

Not everything is under our control. Coaching can help separate what is within a leader’s control from what they can influence, and turn a large problem into choices and actions that are still available.

Evidence note

Based on an initial analysis of 48 clients and more than 100 coaching sessions, professional HR experience, and de-identified recurring themes including challenges with a manager above, difficult conversations, delegation and the gap between decision and action. No client, manager or organization is identifiable.

01

Not everything is under our control

In coaching conversations, I have repeatedly heard situations such as: ‘My manager controls too much,’ ‘I do not have enough authority,’ ‘My team is not accountable,’ or ‘The structure gets in the way.’ Often, those descriptions are real.

The structure may be unclear, a manager above may intervene heavily, or the system may reinforce the same behaviour the organization later complains about. But my coaching question is usually not who is to blame. It is: given these conditions, what can you do?

02

The circle of control and the circle of influence

Some things are within our control: what we say, what we ask, which decisions we make within our role, where we set a boundary, and which conversation we stop postponing.

Other things are not under our direct control, but they are still within our influence. We may not be able to redesign the structure, but we may be able to clarify a problem with data. We may not be able to change our manager, but we can change how we interact, ask, negotiate or make a case. We may not be able to transform the culture at once, but we can start a different pattern inside our own team.

If we focus only on what we fully control, our room to move can become too small. If we focus only on what is outside our control, we can become powerless very quickly. Coaching can help separate the two.

03

I have seen this distinction repeatedly in coaching

In the initial analysis of more than 100 coaching sessions, challenges with a manager above were among the recurring themes. But the answer was not always that the manager needed to change.

Sometimes the client needed to set a clearer boundary. Sometimes they needed to have a conversation they had postponed for months. Sometimes they needed to stop guessing what the manager thought and ask directly. Sometimes they needed to make a clearer request or move forward with a decision that was already within their own authority.

The external conditions could remain the same while the client’s way of showing up changed. That shift could also change the relationship and the space around them.

04

Change one link and the system may respond

A system is not only structure and process. It is also made of relationships and repeated behavioural patterns. When one person changes how they act in a relationship, the other people in that system face a new set of conditions.

If a manager who used to do everything themselves starts delegating clearly and following up consistently, the relationship with the team changes. If someone who routinely avoided a difficult conversation enters it directly and respectfully, the relational pattern changes. If a manager who always waited for instruction starts bringing analysis and a proposal, the way that manager is seen may change too.

This does not mean one person can repair every broken system. They cannot. But they can change one link in the system, and that change may affect the links around it.

05

Agency begins here

One recurring pattern in the coaching evidence is the gap between decision and action. A person may know what they want and even know what they need to do, but change can stay at the level of intention until the next step, ownership, timing and support become clear.

That is why one question has become important to me: what does this conversation return to the client’s field of action? Not whether the whole problem is solved, but whether the client can now see what they need to accept, what they can change, what they can influence, and what their next step is.

06

The system may not change, but the choice can become clearer

Sometimes the manager responds differently, the team becomes more accountable, or decision boundaries become clearer. Sometimes none of those things change.

Even then, the client has better information. They know what is still available to them, what is genuinely outside their control, and whether the next move is to negotiate, continue, change roles or leave the environment.

For me, this is one of the practical values of coaching: turning a problem that feels large and outside someone’s control into choices that are still available to them.
