Human & AI

Better context produces more useful AI

AI becomes more valuable when it extends grounded experience and traceable knowledge instead of manufacturing expertise from a thin prompt.

22 August 20267 min readAnalytical note

Core question

What would AI need to know to be useful in this context?

Evidence note

Developed from Mehdi's reflection on Colin Scotland's Coaching.com presentation about using AI to amplify a coaching business, combined with evidence-first knowledge principles.

01

The prompt is not the whole context

A polished prompt can improve an answer, but it cannot replace the experience, distinctions, evidence and judgment that make an answer worth using. When the underlying context is thin, AI tends to produce work that is plausible and generic.

For a coach or HR leader, the useful context includes how they think, the boundaries of their practice, the sources behind their claims and the patterns they have actually observed.

02

Build the stack before scaling the output

A professional knowledge system starts with evidence. Evidence becomes organized knowledge. Repeated observations may become patterns. Patterns can support frameworks. Only then should the system scale those ideas into articles, questions, videos or tools.

This sequence helps AI extend expertise without pretending to be its source.

03

Traceability changes the standard

Every public asset should be able to answer a simple question: where did this come from? The answer may be practice, research, a named source or a clearly labelled reflection. If the origin is uncertain, the claim should remain a draft.

The goal is not more content. It is a system that can produce useful work without losing the human experience and judgment that give the work its value.

04

A minimum responsible workflow

Capture the source before asking AI to expand it. Separate observation from interpretation, preserve the context in which an idea was formed and record what would make the claim unsafe or misleading outside that context.

Then use AI for retrieval, comparison, structure and adaptation. Human review remains responsible for truth, confidentiality, professional boundaries and the decision to publish.

Questions to carry forward

  1. 01What evidence is this output built on?
  2. 02Which parts are observed, which are sourced and which are inference?
  3. 03What context would another person need before using this asset responsibly?
